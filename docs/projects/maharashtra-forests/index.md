# Van Maharashtra · Forests and industrial projects

Maharashtra's forest cover in 3D from 2021 to 2050. An illustrative pipeline of mines, plants, roads, railways, dams, industrial estates and ports takes forest land, thins the forest around it and breaks the corridors between tiger reserves. Three scenarios show how much depends on how projects are sited and mitigated.

## The 2021 baseline

| Class (canopy density) | Area | Share of the state |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Very dense forest (over 70%) | 8,734 km² | 2.8% |
| Moderately dense forest (40–70%) | 20,589 km² | 6.7% |
| Open forest (10–40%) | 21,475 km² | 7.0% |
| **Forest cover** | **50,798 km²** | **16.5%** of 307,713 km² |

The state is cut into 8,867 cells of about 6 × 6 km, each holding a share of the three classes. The main tracts are:

- **East and north:** Gadchiroli, Chandrapur–Tadoba, Gondia–Bhandara (Navegaon–Nagzira), Pench and Umred, Melghat, Yavatmal–Kinwat, and the Satpura in Nandurbar and Jalgaon.
- **West:** Nashik–Thane–Palghar, the Sahyadri from Igatpuri to Tillari, and the Konkan mangroves.

The Nagpur division holds about half the state's forest.

## What happens each year

1. **Clearing:** each project takes its forest land from the cells it sits on, or along its route for roads and railways.
2. **Thinning:** for ten years, the forest around a project loses density from edge effects, new access, fuelwood and grazing. Very dense forest becomes moderately dense, and moderately dense becomes open. Some open forest is cleared outright for new roads and settlement.
3. **Background:** dense forest thins slowly statewide. In the Avoid scenario, restoration also adds open cover.
4. **Compensatory afforestation:** an equal area is planted after a lag.
   - Saplings are not forest cover.
   - A plantation counts as open forest after 5 years (if it survives) and as moderately dense after 20.
   - Under business as usual it is planted on degraded land far away, in Marathwada, which is why that division gains cover while the others lose it.
5. **Corridors:** each is scored against 2021 from the forest along it. Roads and railways cut it (less so with wildlife crossings), and so do mines and plants nearby.

## Three scenarios

| By 2050 | Business as usual | Avoid and mitigate | High growth |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Forest cover | 49,320 km² (**−1,478**) | 52,646 km² (+1,848) | 45,773 km² (**−5,025**) |
| Share of the state | 16.0% | 17.1% | 14.9% |
| Very dense forest | **−20.6%** | −3.3% | **−38.1%** |
| Cleared directly for projects | 286 km² | 81 km² | 612 km² |
| Thinned around projects | 3,418 km² | 196 km² | 7,883 km² |
| Open forest lost to access and settlement | 1,350 km² | 77 km² | 4,008 km² |
| Forest carbon | −24 Mt C | +7 Mt C | −61 Mt C |
| Projects built | 24 | 18 (6 dropped) | 48 |
| Tadoba–Gadchiroli–Indravati corridor | 69 / 100 | 88 / 100 | 56 / 100 |

The land cleared directly is small: about 0.6% of the state's forest under business as usual. The damage is mostly elsewhere:

- dense forest thinning around each project;
- the open forest lost to the roads and settlements that follow;
- corridors broken where projects cluster, as in the Wardha valley and the Gadchiroli hills.

Plantations add area but not old forest. Total cover can look steady while the densest forest disappears.

## The project pipeline

24 illustrative projects from 2022 to 2036 cover iron-ore, limestone and bauxite mines, open-cast coal, plants and an ash pond, highways and railways, dams, industrial estates and a port.

Each has a year, the forest land it seeks, and what the Avoid scenario does with it:

- **drop** it if it is in a tiger reserve, corridor or the Western Ghats;
- **shift** it to non-forest land;
- **shrink** it by 40%;
- or add **wildlife crossings** to a road or railway.

The Projects tab lists them with the forest each actually took. Clicking one flies to it, or to its close-up site.

## The law, in brief

- **Diversion approval:** using forest land for non-forest purposes needs central approval under the Forest (Conservation) Act, 1980. It was renamed the Van (Sanrakshan Evam Samvardhan) Adhiniyam in 2023.
- **Payments:** user agencies pay for compensatory afforestation and the net present value of the forest lost, into funds managed under the Compensatory Afforestation Fund Act, 2016 (CAMPA).
- **Community rights:** the Forest Rights Act, 2006 recognises the rights of forest-dwelling communities.
- **Wildlife clearances:** tiger reserves, sanctuaries and eco-sensitive zones need these in addition.
