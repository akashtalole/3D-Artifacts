    Frame loop¶

    flowchart LR
  clock[Sim clock<br/>t × warp] --> orbit[Orbit.state t<br/>Sun · GMST · eclipse]
  orbit --> light[Sun & Earth-shine<br/>lights]
  orbit --> earth[Earth rotation<br/>& star field]
  orbit --> arrays[SARJ / BGA /<br/>TRRJ pointing]
  clock --> player[MissionPlayer<br/>tracks & scalars]
  player --> robots[MBS → Canadarm2 IK<br/>ERA IK]
  robots --> crew[Crew pose<br/>attach · orient · animate]
  crew --> payloads[Payloads]
  crew --> tethers[Tether routing]
  payloads --> render
  tethers --> render[Two-pass render<br/>far Earth · near station]
  render --> hud[HUD & ground track]

    Two-pass rendering¶

    The Earth is 6,786 km from the station's centre, but the camera can sit 5 cm from a helmet. One depth buffer cannot cover both ranges, so each frame is drawn twice through an EffectComposer:

    1. Far pass: Earth, atmosphere, stars and the Sun, with a camera whose near/far planes are 20 km / 120,000 km.
    2. Near pass: the station and crew, with near/far 5 cm / 4 km. The depth buffer is cleared first and the colour buffer is kept.
    3. Bloom and output: an Unreal bloom pass for the Sun and specular glints, then ACES tone mapping.

    Both cameras share position and orientation, so the two layers line up exactly.

    Orbit & lighting¶

    Orbit.state(t) returns everything the frame needs:

    • Position: ECI position and velocity directions of a circular orbit, Rz(Ω)·Rx(i)·[cos u, sin u, 0].
    • Station frame (LVLH): x = velocity, y = radial, z = x × y (starboard).
    • Sun: solar longitude from the date, projected into the station frame.
    • Beta angle and eclipse: β = asin(ŝ · ĥ). The Sun is eclipsed when its angle from nadir is less than Earth's angular radius, asin(R⊕ / r) ≈ 69.7°, which also gives analytic sunrise and sunset countdowns.
    • Earth orientation: M = L · Rz(GMST) · P turns the textured sphere so the real coastline sits under the station.

    Sunlight fades over about 1.5° around the limb with a warm tint, so orbital sunsets look as fast as they are in reality (about 10 seconds at 1×).

    Mission timeline¶

    MissionPlayer compiles a mission (authored in minutes) into tracks:

    • Vector tracks (crew, arm end-effectors, payloads) use eased interpolation between keys. The segment direction drives head-first translation and the hand-over-hand limb cycle.
    • Scalar tracks drive the MBS rail position, hatch angle, iROSA visibility and roll-out fraction.
    • Attachments make one track follow another (ev2 on arm, battery on ev1). At compile time the player inserts keys at each attachment boundary, so pick-ups and hand-offs are continuous.
    • Live carriers: at run time, attached objects follow the solved arm tip rather than the requested one. If a target is out of reach, the astronaut stays on the foot restraint instead of floating free.

    Safety tethers¶

    Each crew member's tether starts at the airlock. As they translate, a routing point is added every ~16 m and removed when they pass back over it, so the yellow line follows the route they took. Seeking rebuilds the routing by replaying the path.

    Performance notes¶

    • Truss lattices and solar-array masts are InstancedMesh batches (thousands of members, a handful of draw calls).
    • Textures are generated on canvases at start-up. The only downloads are three.js, fonts and three Earth maps.
    • HQ toggles shadow maps (4096² sun shadow), bloom and device-pixel-ratio rendering.
